1. Corpus snapshot

The report shows that the corpus is heterogeneous in style composition, transcription stability, and error behaviour.

  • The training corpus contains 2,068 documents, with 489 held out as test documents.
  • The training material contains 2,335,229 normalised reference characters, 567,449 tokens, and 69,928 reference lines.
  • The mean normalised CER is 0.16, while the median normalised CER is 0.14. The higher mean indicates that the error distribution in the corpus is not uniformly distributed but is actually skewed with a high-error tail.
  • The mean WER is significantly higher than the comparative CER at 0.37, suggesting that character-level divergence compounds into substantial word-level disruption.
  • Issues are overwhelmingly concentrated in the metrics requiring GT-HTR comparison, accounting for 98.57% of all logged issues. By contrast, surface anomalies account for only 0.16% of all logged issues, and orthographic violations for the remaining 1.27%.

Overall interpretation

  • The main problem is not surface cleaning or orthographic errors. The majority of errors occur at document structural level and are surfaced initially through HTR-GT comparison.
  • The difference between mean and median CER, together with the concentration statistics discussed further down, shows that instability is unevenly distributed across documents.
  • The corpus should therefore be treated as a mixed-quality, style-conditioned training environment, not as a single homogeneous dataset.

Fine-tuning takeaway

  • The fine-tuning pipeline should not ingest the corpus uniformly.
  • The first operational split should be by style.
  • Within a given style, metrics such as CER, IDR, and continuity should be used to allocate documents to a risk banding, as has been achieved through the construction of the style-stratifed risk index
  • Low-risk documents should form the stable supervision baseline; high-risk documents should be isolated for review, stress-testing, or later-stage robustness training.

RAG takeaway

  • For RAG, missing content and loss of correspondence between HTR and GT should be the main areas of focus.
  • Consider attaching document-level metadata on quality along with the transcriptions as they are ingested into the RAG pipeline. This would allow documents to be filtered, prioritised, or flagged according to their assessed transcription risk.

2. Corpus shape and issue context

  • The corpus is heavily weighted towards Procesal, with 893 documents, or 43.18% of the training corpus.
  • Encadenada contributes 579 documents (28.00%), while Italica Cursiva contributes 397 documents (19.20%).
  • The two Redonda-derived categories are much smaller: Pseudo Redonda has 122 documents (5.90%) and Redonda has 77 documents (3.72%).
  • Procesal is not only the largest style by document count; it also contains 1,332,584 characters, approximately 45.9% of the style-level character total reported in the geometry table.
  • Line geometry differs by style. Redonda has the longest median line length at 56 characters, followed by Italica Cursiva at 52, Procesal at 46, Pseudo Redonda at 42, and finally Encadenada at 33.
  • Issue burden is not proportional to document count:
    • Procesal accounts for 43.18% of documents but 47.23% of logged issues, making it overweight in error representation by +4.05 percentage points.
    • Italica Cursiva accounts for 19.20% of documents but 22.10% of issues, making it overweight by +2.90 percentage points.
    • Encadenada accounts for 28.00% of documents but only 22.56% of issues, making it underweight by -5.43 percentage points.

Overall interpretation

  • The errors observed across styles vary markedly.
  • Procesal is the most consequential style operationally because it is both large and issue-heavy.
  • Encadenada is large but relatively underweight in logged issues, meaning that it may provide more stable material than its raw size alone would suggest.
  • Redonda is small, but its longer line structure and low error profile make it a useful clean reference category.

Fine-tuning takeaway

  • Do not sample only by document count. Weighting should consider corpus share, character volume, issue burden, and style-specific stability.
  • Procesal requires particular care: any model trained naively on the corpus will see a large amount of procesal material and a disproportionate share of its instability.
  • Encadenada may be useful as a comparatively stable large-style pool, but this should be balanced against its lower continuity score in the style overview.

RAG takeaway

  • Style should be retained as document and chunk metadata in the vector store.
  • Chunking policies may need to differ by style because line length and transcription stability differ materially.
  • Redonda and Pseudo Redonda appear to provide comparatively reliable foundations for retrieval applications.
  • Procesal and Italica Cursiva may require more rigorous assessment and review prior to downstream use.

  • Use Pseudo Redonda carefully: its median is promising, but the P90 CER of 0.488 means high-risk documents must be filtered.
  • Consider stratifying Procesal because it is too large and variable to treat as a single, homogeneous group.
  • Encadenada should not be assumed to be safe simply because it is underweight in logged issues: its median continuity of 0.143377 is low and should be considered as a warning signal.

RAG takeaway

  • Redonda and lower-risk Pseudo Redonda are likely to produce cleaner retrieval chunks.
  • Encadenada requires special caution for RAG because low continuity suggests fragmented aligned structure, even where line structure appears stable.
  • High-P90 styles should use stricter chunk validation because document-level medians may hide severely disrupted local regions.

4. Line-structure integrity and segmentation risk

  • Line-structure integrity is generally strong across the corpus: each style has at least 99.18% stable adjusted line structure.
  • Redonda is perfectly stable on this measure: 100.00% stable line structure and 0.00% severe line instability.
  • Procesal has the highest unmatched HTR blank incidence at 1.01% of documents, followed by Italica Cursiva at 0.76% and Encadenada at 0.69%.
  • Severe line instability is rare in the current corpus but present in Encadenada (0.17%) and Procesal (0.34%).
  • Moderate instability is minimally observable in Pseudo Redonda (0.82%), Italica Cursiva (0.50%), Procesal (0.34%), and Encadenada (0.17%).
  • Note that this corpus has now been specifically cleaned to remove line differences between HTR and GT so the high levels of line structure integrity are not surprising
  • However, this observation may not hold true as additional documents are added to the corpus.

Overall interpretation

  • Most instability in the corpus is not caused by simple line-count mismatches.
  • However, the small number of line-structure failures is operationally significant because such failures can disproportionately damage training alignment and chunk boundaries.
  • Line-structure risk should therefore be used as an escalation flag rather than as a dominant explanatory metric.

Fine-tuning takeaway

  • Documents with moderate or severe line instability should be reviewed or excluded from the cleanest supervision pools.
  • Because most documents have stable line structure, adjusted line delta should remain a low-weight component of the index rather than a dominant score.
  • The combination high CER + high IDR + low continuity + line instability should be treated as a strong exclusion or review signal.

RAG takeaway

  • Line-structure failures are rare but highly relevant for chunking.
  • Documents flagged as review_line_structure or monitor_line_structure should not be chunked blindly.
  • For RAG, line-structure metadata should be attached to chunk records so that retrieval confidence can be reduced for structurally unstable source documents.

RAG takeaway

  • High-burden documents should receive lower retrieval confidence until manually reviewed or chunk-validated.
  • For small clean styles, outlier detection is especially important: a few bad documents can dominate the retrieval-risk profile of the whole style.
  • Error concentration tables should feed directly into the RAG exclusion or warning list.

6. Recurring Error Patterns

  • Character confusions are dominated in many styles by case and graphemic variants:
    • Encadenada: L→l occurs 239 times (20.43% of style character confusions), followed by E→e (185) and D→d (108).
    • Italica Cursiva: L→l (136), S→s (133), and Y→y (130) dominate.
    • Procesal: E→e is especially prominent with 445 instances (21.67%), followed by L→l (307).
    • Redonda shows a more historically meaningful pattern: c→ç occurs 16 times (12.70%), alongside e→E and L→l.
  • Word-level confusions often involve historically meaningful orthographic variation:
    • Encadenada includes senor→señor and año→ano at 6 instances each.
    • Italica Cursiva includes çiudad→ciudad (6) and seisçientos→seiscientos (5).
    • Procesal includes ciudad→çiudad (12), anos→años (8), and senor→señor (8).
    • Redonda includes principal→prinçipal (3) and related cedilla forms.
  • Bigram patterns occur less frequently than character- and word-level confusions and therefore provide supporting rather than primary evidence for understanding transcription behaviour.

Overall interpretation

  • The confusion evidence shows structured error behaviour rather than random noise.
  • Many recurrent errors involve historical orthographic variation, including cedilla forms, tilde behaviour, and case substitutions.
  • The outputs do not seem to indicate that the most frequent character-level confusions change lexical context, which would have more of an impact on search and retrieval behaviour.

Fine-tuning takeaway

  • Recurrent confusion patterns should be treated as explicit evaluation targets: improvements should be assessed not only through aggregate metrics such as CER but also through reductions in systematic error classes.
  • The findings suggest that not all transcription errors warrant equal intervention. Frequent orthographic variants and case substitutions may be less consequential than errors that remove, introduce, or replace lexical content.
  • Training interventions should therefore prioritise error patterns that affect textual correspondence and lexical integrity rather than focusing solely on surface-form variation.

RAG takeaway

  • Many recurrent confusions involve historical orthographic variants that preserve the underlying lexical item and can therefore be accommodated through retrieval normalisation and query expansion.
  • Historical variants such as senor/señor, ciudad/çiudad, and anos/años can be addressed through retrieval equivalence rules.
  • Retrieval performance is likely to be affected more by errors that alter lexical content or remove information entirely than by frequent character-level variants that preserve meaning.

    • Procesal insertion peak: 0-10% line position and 70-80% document position.
    • Redonda insertion peak: 0-10% line position and 80-90% document position.
  • The error topology summary shows that substitution events are much less frequent than deletion and insertion events.

Overall interpretation

  • The topology section is one of the strongest pieces of evidence that transcription error is structurally organised.
  • The repeated deletion peak at 90-100% for both line and document positions suggests a systematic end-region failure pattern, not random character-level noise.
  • The combination of late-document deletions and line-start insertions suggests that once the HTR loses alignment with the source text, subsequent regions become increasingly misaligned and accumulate additional transcription errors.

Fine-tuning takeaway

  • Future model evaluation should test whether fine-tuning reduces late-document and line-end deletion concentration.
  • Topology should be used as an evaluation diagnostic, not as part of the main risk index.
  • This is because many topology signals are the downstream result of broader transcription failures already captured by e.g. continuity and insertion-deletion measures, incorporating topology into the composite score risks over-weighting related forms of error.
  • The principal value of topology metrics lies in helping to explain how transcription failures arise and propagate.
  • Training subsets should preserve variation in the positional distribution of errors so that models are exposed to a range of transcription behaviours rather than predominantly well-aligned regions of text.

RAG takeaway

  • Chunks drawn from late-document regions should be treated with caution, especially in styles where deletion peaks are extremely high.
  • Chunk overlap may need to increase near unstable document regions, particularly toward document endings.
  • The RAG pipeline should consume topology profiles as metadata, enabling retrieval systems to distinguish between passages originating from relatively stable regions of text versus those drawn from regions with elevated transcription risk.

8. Operational governance and subset construction

  • The risk index is intentionally narrow and uses only four metrics:
    • CER, weight 0.35;
    • IDR, weight 0.30;
    • continuity risk, weight 0.25;
    • adjusted line delta, weight 0.10.
  • Risk bands are assigned within style, producing approximately one third high, medium, and low risk documents per style:
    • Encadenada: 193 high, 193 medium, 193 low.
    • Procesal: 298 high, 298 medium, 297 low.
    • Redonda: 26 high, 26 medium, 25 low.
  • The highest-risk documents show that risk is often driven by extreme CER, IDR, and low continuity even when line structure remains stable.
  • For example, the top redonda-risk document has CER 0.372093, IDR 0.186047, and continuity 0.000000, despite stable line structure.

Overall interpretation

  • The index is not a complete description of document quality. It is an operational governance instrument.
  • Its strength is that it combines overall error, drift, coherence, and segmentation risk without absorbing every diagnostic signal.
  • This makes the index more defensible than a broad composite that includes every available metric.

Fine-tuning takeaway

  • Use low-risk documents as the cleanest supervision pool within each style.
  • Use medium-risk documents for robustness and curriculum-learning experiments.
  • Use high-risk documents for review, exclusion testing, and stress-testing, not for initial stable fine-tuning.
  • Because risk is assigned within style, every style contributes candidate low-risk and high-risk material.

RAG takeaway

  • High-risk documents should not be indexed without warning metadata or chunk-level verification.
  • Risk score should become a document-level retrieval confidence prior.
  • Low-risk documents can seed the first reliable retrieval corpus while high-risk material is reviewed.

10. Critical cautions and academic interpretation

Critical cautions

  • The topology section currently uses issue events per document. It is highly informative, but should not be overinterpreted as direct semantic damage without further validation.
  • Some recurrent confusions are historically plausible orthographic variants rather than straightforward model failures.
  • The risk index is style-relative. A high-risk Redonda document is high-risk within redonda, not necessarily worse than every Procesal document in absolute terms.
  • Line-structure instability is rare, so it is best treated as an escalation signal rather than a major corpus-wide explanation.
  • RAG implications remain partly prospective until the actual chunking strategy is known.

Overall interpretation

  • The report's strongest contribution is not simply that it provides more metrics than CER/WER.
  • Its contribution is that it shows how historical HTR quality can be governed as a multi-dimensional, style-conditioned, structurally uneven data problem.
  • This places the work closer to data-centric ML and corpus governance than to conventional OCR benchmarking alone.

Fine-tuning takeaway

  • The next experimental step should compare fine-tuning outcomes across curated subset regimes: clean-only, balanced risk, high-risk excluded, and style-specific curriculum.
  • Evaluation should report not only CER/WER but also whether IDR, continuity, deletion topology, and recurrent confusions improve.

RAG takeaway

  • The RAG system should begin with document-level quality metadata and later move to chunk-level scoring once chunking is finalised.
  • The most important RAG risks are omission, low continuity, line/document positional instability, and entity-bearing lexical corruption.
